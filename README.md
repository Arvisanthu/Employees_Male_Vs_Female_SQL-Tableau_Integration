## 📊 **Employee Gender and Salary Analysis Dashboard**

### 1️⃣ **Employee Gender Distribution (1990–2002)**

* The total number of employees increased **steadily** from 1990 to 2002.
* **Male employees** consistently made up about **60%** of the workforce, while **female employees** accounted for around **40%**.
* The gender ratio remained **stable** over the years, showing balanced hiring practices without drastic gender disparity.

📈 **Insight:** There has been continuous workforce growth with consistent gender representation — approximately 3 male employees for every 2 female employees.

---

### 2️⃣ **Average Annual Employee Salary (1990–2002)**

* Both male and female average salaries showed a **gradual increase** over time.
* **Male employees** earned slightly higher on average compared to females, though the **gap narrowed** after 2000.
* Salaries rose from around **$50,000 in 1990** to nearly **$65,000 by 2002**.

💡 **Insight:** The organization maintained steady salary growth for both genders, moving toward pay equity in recent years.

---

### 3️⃣ **Number of Managers per Department (1990–2000)**

* The number of active managers **increased sharply** until 1996–1998, peaking at around **65 managers**, and then slightly **declined** afterward.
* Both male and female managers increased proportionally during the growth phase.
* After 1998, there was a drop in female management representation.

📉 **Insight:** The company expanded its management layer during the mid-1990s, followed by slight downsizing or restructuring around 1999–2000.

---

### 4️⃣ **Average Employee Salary by Department and Gender (Since 1990)**

* Salaries are **comparable across genders** within most departments.
* **Finance** and **Sales** departments show **slightly higher averages** (~$65K–$70K).
* **Human Resources** and **Quality Management** have the lowest averages (~$58K).
* Minimal salary gap observed between male and female employees across departments.

📊 **Insight:** Salary distribution across departments is equitable, with higher pay observed in client-facing or revenue-generating roles like Sales and Finance.

---

### 🧠 **Overall Summary**

* Workforce grew steadily with consistent gender proportions.
* Average salaries rose over the years with near gender parity by 2002.
* Management positions peaked in the late 1990s, then stabilized.
* Salary equity is well-maintained across departments, showing organizational fairness and balanced compensation strategies.
